# Style and communication notes

Persistent preferences for any writing that goes out under Willard's name, especially to Nepali business contacts.

## Tone

- Nepali business culture is formal and courteous. Default to that register.
- "Thank you" rather than "Thanks". Same for "I would" rather than "I'd" where it does not sound stiff.
- English is most contacts' second language. They speak it well, often better than the writer. That is not a reason to over-simplify or be patronizing. It is a reason to be clear and avoid jargon, idiom, and consultant filler.

## Hard rules

- **Never use em dashes (—).** No exceptions. Use periods, commas, parentheses, semicolons, or a colon instead.
- **Avoid corporate consultant phrases.** Examples to avoid: "a much faster shared vocabulary than describing capabilities in the abstract", "let us double-click on that", "circle back", "synergies", "value proposition".
- Do not name-drop the writer's expertise. The work speaks. Let the recipient infer.

## Formatting

- Plain paragraphs, not bullet-heavy unless the content genuinely is a list (dates, named items).
- One idea per sentence where possible. Short paragraphs are easier to read in a second language.

## Sign-off

- "Kind regards," is the preferred close for business email.
- Signature block: name, title, company, phone with country code.
