## 1. Document metadata

- **Full title:** *"Nepal Green Finance Taxonomy"* (also styled "Nepal Rastra Bank 2024"; cover, p. 1). Referred to in-text as the "Green Finance Taxonomy" or "guideline on green taxonomy" (Governor's Foreword, p. 2).
- **Publisher:** Nepal Rastra Bank (NRB), Banks and Financial Institution Regulation Department; developed with technical/financial assistance from Alliance for Financial Inclusion (AFI) and consultants Oxford Policy Management (OPM). Peer review: Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (BSP) and Bank Negara Malaysia (Acknowledgment, p. 4).
- **Version marker:** cover page reads only "NEPAL RASTRA BANK 2024"; the file itself is `Nepal-Green-Finance-Taxonomy-2024-V1.pdf`. Annex 4i timeline shows "GFT finalized and Accepted by NRB Management: December 2023; GFT Published for public Consultation: January 2024" (p. 152).
- **Applicability:** binding on the "wider financial sector actors: … Banking and Financial Institutions, Capital, and Insurance market community of practice", including private sector, MSMEs, pension funds, trust funds and other financial sector actors (Exec Summary "Target users", p. 9; s. 1.3 "Users of the Green Finance Taxonomy" — Class A, B, C, D BFIs plus Nepal Infrastructure Bank Limited; SEBON-supervised merchant banks; NIA-supervised life/non-life/reinsurance/micro-insurance, p. 15).
- **Alignment (voluntary but tracked):** "The classified economic activities are, however, not a mandatory list of investments for the investors. Market actors are encouraged to promote 'green' activities and provide transparent reporting on investments made in 'green', 'amber' or 'red' sectors to receive green finance credentials from the regulators- with evidence that the assets are taxonomy aligned." (Exec Summary "Application", p. 11).

## 2. Core classification framework (verbatim)

### 2.1 Colour system (Table 3, s. 3.2.2 "Classification of Activities", p. 27)

| Classification | Definition | Decision Making |
|---|---|---|
| **Green or Transformative** | "Economic activities and/or financial activities which adheres to core principles and help achieve climate and environmental objectives" | **Investment Ready** |
| **Amber or Transitional** | "Economic activities and/or financial products that are on a transition pathway to becoming green and need some remedial measures to be able to meet climate and environment objectives fully." | **Investment after suggestion and adoption of remedial measures** |
| **Red** | "Economic activities and/or financial products that are not compliant with environmental objectives." | **Discourage Activities- Encourage reshaping of investments.** |

(Footnote 19, p. 27: "Remedial measures are risk mitigation measures that includes activities that will reduce the risk of the certain investments and harness opportunities.")

### 2.2 Four Core Environmental Principles (Chapter 2.3, pp. 20–22)

| Code | Principle | Verbatim purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Climate Change **Adaptation** | "process of adjustments to actual or expected changes to the climate and its effects … meet objectives … set by Nepal's Climate Change Policy, National Adaptation Plan (NAP), Local Adaptation Plans of Action (LAPA)". Contributions: reduce vulnerability & risks; harness beneficial opportunities. (p. 20) |
| **M** | Climate Change **Mitigation** | "align with Nepal's aspiration to minimise emissions and sustainably achieve net-zero emissions by 2045 as envisioned by Nepal's Long-Term Strategy to Net Zero Emissions and Nepal's Nationally Determined Contributions (NDC) … limiting global temperature rise to no more than 1.5°C in alignment with the Paris Agreement." Contributions: reduce GHG, avoid GHG via low-carbon/resilient economy, enhance sinks. (pp. 20–21) |
| **N** | **Natural Resource Conservation and Management** | "healthy ecosystems and biodiversity … align with Nepal's National Climate Change Policy 2019, National Forest Policy 2018, Forest Act 2019 and Regulations 2022, National Parks and Wildlife Conservation Act 1973, Environment Protection Act 2019 and Regulations 2020." Contributions: maintain ecological diversity; conserve biodiversity for future generations; harness green jobs. (p. 21) |
| **P** | **Pollution Prevention and Control** | "align with National Environment Policy 2019, Environment Protection Act 2019 and its Regulations 2020 and environment standards." Contributions: prevent/reduce air/water/noise/land pollution; improve air/water/soil quality; prevent adverse impact on human health & environment; avoid emissions of short/long-lived climate pollutants; proper waste management & circular economy. (p. 21) |

Every activity in Annex 2 is tagged with one or more of these letters in parentheses, e.g. "(A, M, N)".

### 2.3 DNSH — verbatim conditions (Table 1, s. 2.3, p. 22)

| Environmental Objective | Conditions for causing "significant harms" |
|---|---|
| Climate Change Adaptation (A) | "Where that activity leads to an increased adverse impact of the current climate and the expected future climate, on the activity itself or on people, nature or assets including maladaptation practices." |
| Climate Change Mitigation (M) | "Where that activity leads to significant greenhouse gas emissions" |
| Natural Resource Management and Conservation (N) | "Activity is detrimental to the • good condition and resilience of the ecosystem. • conservation status of habitat and species, and • ecological potential of the natural resources." |
| Pollution Prevention and Control (P) | "Where the activity leads to a significant increase in emissions of pollutants into air, water or land as compared with the situation before the activity started." |

**Structural note (critical for our diff):** NRB does **not** provide a discrete named library of DNSH check cards (as our `dnsh.ts` implies). DNSH is expressed as (i) the four "significant harm" *conditions* in Table 1, plus (ii) explicit "red" bullets in each activity's cell. There is one shared "Social and Inclusion Aspects" test (s. 2.3, p. 23) with three protections: "Protection of human rights; Prevention from forced labour and protection of children's rights; Inclusive and targeted measures for local communities, prioritising vulnerable and marginalized populations." Our named DNSH cards (environmental_flow, resettlement_discharged, biodiversity_offset, cumulative_basin_impact, quarry_rehabilitation, land_use_conflict, seismic_assessment) are **Jana editorial synthesis** — not verbatim from GFT.

### 2.4 Decision-tree logic (verbatim, s. 3.2.2, pp. 27–28)

> "An investment can be found to be either 'green or transformative', 'amber or transitional', or 'red' using the definition illustrated in Table 3. In this evaluation, it is important to evaluate how the investment contributes to the objectives of the taxonomy. Certain thresholds may apply for the 'Amber or Transitional' category, which will help determine whether the investment is aligned with the category. The classification system starts with the following questions, as per Figure 4 (defined above). Does the investment contribute to the core principles?
>
> - If yes, does the investment significantly harm the core principles or the broader environment?
>   - If no, the investment can be classified as 'Green or Transformational' with focus on inclusion.
>   - If yes, does the investment contribute to taking remedial action against the harmful practices?
>     - If no, the investment can be classified as 'Red.'
>     - If yes, the investment can be classified as 'Green or Transformational.'
> - If not, does the investment significantly harm the core principles or the broader environment?
>   - If no, the investment can be classified as 'Amber or Transitional.'
>   - If yes, does the investment contribute to taking remedial action against the harmful practices?
>     - If no, the investment will be classified as 'Red.'
>     - If yes, the investment will be classified as 'Amber or Transitional.'"

### 2.5 ESRM-before-Taxonomy rule (verbatim)

> "For all economic sectors, investment proposal assessment should be carried out following Annex 3, adhering to the principles and economic activities classification referred to in Annex 2. 'Do No Significant Harm' (DNSH) remains central to these guiding core principles. **In the case of BFIs, Steps 1 and 2 of the Environment and Social Risk Management (ESRM) guidelines shall be applied first and then the taxonomy in terms of classifying activities.**" (s. 3.2.2, p. 26 — emphasis added; this is the paragraph immediately above Table 3).

Annex 3 Note (p. 141): "For any investment decisions aligned with green finance taxonomy- BFIs, insurance and capital market and other financial sector actors adhere to the ESRM, 2022 guidelines and other relevant sectoral policies and acts."

Exec Summary "Treatment of risk" (p. 11): "Application of green finance taxonomy followed by due diligence procedure of investment. For example, Environmental and Social Risk Management Guidelines, 2022 etc."

### 2.6 Common Ground Rules (s. 3.2.1, pp. 25–26)

Fourteen ground rules govern the whole catalogue. Verbatim numbered list:

1. Climate and environmentally Simple, Measurable, Attainable, Realistic and Timebound (SMART) practices (nutrient, water, energy, soil, irrigation).
2. Sustainable natural resources (flora, fauna, land, water, air, and agriculture) conservation and management practices.
3. Use of clean energy, energy efficiency and energy optimization.
4. Certified products, processes, methods, or tools following national and international standards.
5. National and international codes, standards and practices for building and construction, infrastructures, and applications of input (fertilizer).
6. Local seeds, knowledge, technologies, practices, and environmentally sound products.
7. Nature-based practices and approaches (Nature-based solutions, nature-based tourism).
8. Promoting circular economy, including recycling and recyclable products.
9. Environment and climate-resilient technologies, practices, and products (stress tolerant seed or breeds, climate smart agriculture, green technologies).
10. Technologies for pollution control and prevention (Air, water, noise, soil, radioactive, thermal, litter, heath waste etc.).
11. Any credit facilities, financing instruments, insurance schemes, fintech, and bonds are classified under green activities under the taxonomy, such as sustainable banking, green bonds, and green insurance.
12. Green/sustainable procurement, including hire/purchase.
13. Green R&D: Research, development and innovation in the economic activities that directly contribute to the green transition of society-specific solutions and technologies and basic knowledge.
14. Information communication and technology (ICT) — digital development — Early Warning System (EWS) etc.

### 3.2 Key verbatim sub-sector cells (activities relevant to our current code)

The following sub-sectors are the ones most relevant to the Jana taxonomy activity library. For each I give the **Green / Amber / Red** cell language *verbatim* (with page cite). Where NRB embeds numeric thresholds, they are quoted with units.

#### 3.2.1 Hydroelectricity Production (Annex 2 §7.1, Table 11, pp. 104–105)

**Green (Transformative):**
- "Investment in the promotion of the zero-carbon emitting hydroelectricity production facility with safeguarding and environmental protection measures i.e., Hydropower development with climate risk assessment and inclusion and application of risk mitigation measures (A, M, N)"
- "Hydroelectricity generation plant that meets criteria (a) and (c) OR (b) and (c):
   a) The electricity generation facility is a run-of-river plant and does not have an artificial reservoir.
   b) the power density of the electricity generation facility is above 5 W/m².
   c) the life cycle GHG emissions from the generation of electricity from hydropower, are lower than **100 gCO₂e/kWh**. The life cycle GHG emissions are calculated using ISO 14067:2018, ISO 14064-1:2018 or the G-res tool. Quantified life cycle GHG emissions are verified by an independent third-party entire facility <100 gCO₂e/kWh (M, N, P)"
- "Decarbonization and energy storage: pumped storage, electricity storage, including the grid-scale battery storage."
- "Site-specific assessment, carried out by an accredited assessor to meet the requirements of Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)/Initial Environment Assessment (IEE) as per Nepal's Environment Protection Act and Regulations. Hydropower developers are also encouraged to meet hydropower criteria of Climate Bonds Initiative including use ESG Gap Analysis tool to meet ESG requirements as suggested by International Hydropower Association. If any significant gaps are identified, an Environmental and Social Action Plan (ESAP) must be established to address those gaps including details on how and when these gaps will be closed. (M, A, N, P)"

**Amber (Transitional):** Generation plant meets criteria (a) and (c) OR (b) and (c):
- a) run-of-river, no artificial reservoir; b) power density > 5 W/m²; **c) Lifecycle GHG emissions from generation by the entire facility: >100 and <425 gCO₂e/kWh**.

**Red:**
- "Hydroelectricity production in protected areas, biodiversity hotspot areas and disaster-prone areas as identified by VRA and disaster mapping."
- "Hydropower generation not meeting mitigation standards as well EIA/IEE and ESG standards."

**Numeric thresholds:** 100 gCO₂e/kWh (Green ceiling); 425 gCO₂e/kWh (Amber ceiling); 5 W/m² power density; no artificial reservoir criterion. **Note NRB does not use MW capacity buckets (small / medium / large) as classification cutoffs — those do not appear in the taxonomy.**

**DNSH-flavoured tests attached (from red bullets and Green language):** protected/biodiversity/disaster area avoidance; EIA/IEE current; ESG Gap Analysis + ESAP; verified lifecycle GHG measurement (ISO 14067, ISO 14064-1, G-res).

**Evidence:** verified LCA report; NEA connection & MoU; EIA/IEE certificate; ESAP; VRA / disaster-mapping overlay.

#### 3.2.2 Electricity Transmission & Distribution (§7.2, p. 106)
Green: dedicated transmission from clean/green hydro, "meet applicable standards stated in this green finance taxonomy". Amber: minimal-impact installation; "Transmission and distribution equipment, including transformers and power lines, with low emissions and environmental degradation." Red: "Expanding transmission and distribution system for coal-powered electricity"; construction that affects protected areas / forests / water resources. Note (p. 106): "As part of the EU sustainable infrastructure policy, climate mitigation transmission infrastructure or equipment must meet criteria such as **no more than 100 gCO₂/kWh of emissions over the next five years**. Also, the EU taxonomy attempts to keep grid emissions below **100 gCO₂e/kWh over a rolling five-year period**." (This is stated as external reference, not as NRB rule.)

#### 3.2.3 Wind Energy (§7.3, pp. 106–107)
Green: energy-efficient wind facilities using clean tech; corresponding studies to minimise risks for birds, livelihoods, environment. Amber: small-community and MSME low-carbon systems; wind-energy storage facilities with mitigation. Red: "Wind energy plant triggers threat to biodiversity including birds, livelihoods, and the environment".

#### 3.2.4 Solar Energy (§7.4, p. 107)
Green: portable solar home systems / mini-grids / stand-alone; utility-scale PV meeting NRB-specified component minimums (footnote 337 gives strict PV component efficiency and degradation floors); "Solar plants that consider minimizing losses to ecosystems and biodiversity and ensuring protection". Amber: solar plants with battery recycling & waste-disposal plans. Red: solar in protected/agricultural/hazard-prone areas without mitigation; "Solar plants that involve significant land use that results in losses in ecosystems and biodiversity".

Numeric solar-PV component floors (footnote 337, p. 107):
- Polycrystalline Si cell minimum photoelectric conversion ≥ 19 %; monocrystalline ≥ 21 %.
- Polycrystalline module ≥ 17 %; monocrystalline module ≥ 17.8 %.
- Thin-film Si-based / CIGS / CdTe modules ≥ 12 % / 14 % / 14 % / 12 %.
- Degradation: poly / mono cell modules first-year ≤ 2.5 % / 3 %; per year ≤ 0.7 %; 25-year cumulative ≤ 20 %. Thin-film: first year ≤ 5 %; per year ≤ 0.4 %; 25 year ≤ 15 %.

#### 3.2.5 Other Electricity & Energy Production (§7.5, p. 108)
Green: energy optimization captive power from renewables (solar, hydro). Amber: renewable electricity + geothermal facilities demonstrating minimised environmental impact and no GHG. Red: "Energy generation from fossil-fuel-based power plants such as coal, and heavy fuel oil." → **fossil-fired power = Red.**

#### 3.2.6 Cement (§5.11, pp. 97–98)
Amber (Transitional):
- "Production facilities, incorporating dry processes, reduced clinker content."
- "Installation of Hybrid Hoffman Kilns (HHK), Tunnel Kilns (TK), and Compressed Stabilized Earth Blocks (CSEB) technologies to replace traditional brick kilns, to transition towards low-carbon technologies in the industry sector."
- "Low-emission technologies for cement industries i.e., Production facilities, incorporating dry processes, reduced clinker content. Promotion of Concrete Hollow Blocks / Interlocking Concrete Block/ lightweight Cellular Concrete/ Clay Brick of Auto Brick factory with efficient kiln. **(At least 60% of total Masonry wall)**"
- "Low-emission technologies for brick and cement industries must demonstrate their low environmental impact"

Red: raw-material exposure/depletion; dust, noise, GHG (esp. CO₂) contamination; risk of resource depletion. **There is no Green column for cement in Annex 2 §5.11** — cement is at best Amber under NRB.

**Note:** "Clinker" is broken out separately as §5.18 (p. 99). Green: none. Amber: "Improving energy efficiency; Switching to alternative fuels; Clinker substitution; and use of emerging and innovative technologies". Red: "High polluting and does not meet environment standards".

Waste-Heat Recovery (WHR) as a specific green criterion is **not** mentioned by name anywhere in the cement or clinker cells — our current `cement-whr` classifier attributes WHR to NRB but the taxonomy actually keys off dry-process kilns, alternative fuels, clinker substitution, and the 60 %-of-masonry auto-brick criterion. WHR is consistent with the spirit of the cell but is a Jana editorial addition.

#### 3.2.7 Green Buildings — Residential (§6.1, p. 100), Non-Residential Commercial (§6.2, pp. 100–101), Real Estate (§12.1 p. 131, §12.2 pp. 131–132)

Green criteria are stated verbatim as compliance with **domestic green building standards** (footnote 303/441: "Nepal Green Building Guidelines and Green Building Codes of the Department of Urban Development and Building Construction (DUDBC)- under preparation.") **or** with:
- LEED (Leadership in Energy and Environmental Design)
- EDGE (Excellent Design for Greater Efficiencies)
- CBI (Climate Bond Initiatives) residential/commercial criteria
- Other internationally recognized green construction codes (footnote 307/445 lists Bangladesh, Sri Lanka, Mongolia taxonomies as examples).

**NRB does not state a numeric energy-savings percent threshold (e.g. ≥20 %) for buildings.** EDGE itself internally uses 20 % as its rating threshold, but the NRB taxonomy does not restate it as an NRB criterion. Our current `green-buildings` activity's `energy_saving_pct ≥ 20 %` criterion is therefore **not verbatim from GFT** — it is inherited from EDGE.

Amber: partially meets domestic/international codes and covers site selection, existing-building consideration, orientation, access roads, hazard/high-priority resource conservation (trees, waterways, animal habitats) — verbatim bullets on pp. 100–101, 131–132.

Red: non-compliance with codes; construction on IUCN Red-List habitats; on arable/high-fertility soil; in disaster-prone areas; in cultural/religious cities; carbon-intensive real estate.

#### 3.2.8 Transport — EVs and charging (§7.6, §9.1, §9.5, §9.10, §17.2)

- **§7.6 Other Renewal Energy including charge station** (p. 108): Green — "Sustainable charging stations rely on renewable energy sources, such as hydro, solar or wind power, to ensure clean energy for EV charging"; "Use of efficient and long-lasting batteries that are critical for sustainable charging stations". Amber — biofuel/biogas facilities. Red — "Charging stations that rely on fossil-fuel-based energy sources (coal, heavy fuel oil)".
- **§9.1 Motor Vehicles; Parts and Accessories** (p. 119): Green — clean-energy motor vehicles manufacturing supply chain. Amber — dedicated manufacturing facilities for vehicles and key components (batteries). Red — non-standard tyre manufacture; overuse of motor vehicle parts with no recycling.
- **§9.5 Rail** (p. 121): Green — all electrified rail infrastructure; rolling stock for non-electrified rail if ≤ 25 % fossil-fuel freight (in tonne/km). Red — coal-run, highly fossil-fuel-intensive rail.
- **§9.10 Mass public Transportation under priority sector** (pp. 124–125): Green — all mass public vehicles with no direct emissions (electric or hydrogen); electric BRT; public walking and cycling. Amber — efficient fossil-fuel or hybrid vehicles meeting universal gCO₂/p-km. Red — "Manufacture and upgrade, Passenger vehicles powered by fossil fuels (e.g., gas, petrol & diesel)".
- **§17.2 Hire Purchase Vehicle — Personal Consumption** (p. 139): Green — "Purchase of Electric Vehicles (EV motor, E-scooters including bicycle). (M); Conversion of petrol/diesel vehicles to an electric vehicle". Amber — "Purchase and/or operation of vehicles for hybrid-engine vehicles (i.e., hybrid car/ jeep)". Red — "Purchase of fossil fuel-run vehicles powered by diesel, gas, and petrol."

Our `ev-transport` classifier is correctly BEV = green, hybrid = amber, but it should cite §17.2 (Consumption Loan) for personal EVs, and §9.10 for mass-transport EVs.

#### 3.2.9 Irrigation (§1.15, pp. 71–72)
Green:
- Smart irrigation coupling sensors + control instruments + irrigation machinery with computer models and meteorological information.
- Solar-powered micro drip.
- Pressurized piped irrigation with remote sensing.
- "Installation and operation of high-efficiency irrigation systems such as Rainwater harvesting systems/ stored-rainwater-based irrigation, drip irrigation systems, sprinkler irrigation, Solar lifting system – solar powered pump irrigation, wind pumps, gravity flow canal (A, M, N)".
- Programmed irrigation at dawn/dusk.
- Managed aquifer recharge + catchment management.
- Farmer-Managed / Agency-Managed Irrigation Systems (FMIS / AMIS).

Amber: re-excavation of traditional ponds; construction of water catchments/storage; small reservoir dams with spillways; plastic ponds; canal-system irrigation; river-based irrigation not following standards; shallow tube wells; energy pricing/rationing controls; land-use regulation.

Red: "Irrigation system that uses Diesel pumps"; "Deep boring"; "Use of shallow tube wells in water-scarce areas where the water table is low"; extraction rate > recharge rate.

#### 3.2.10 Crops & Vegetables (§1.1, pp. 50–55) — organic/climate-smart agriculture
Green: water-smart, energy-smart, nutrient-smart practices; climate-smart villages / diversity farms; organically certified crops; precision nutrient management; SRI; zero-tillage; sustainable land-use management; improved climate-resilient seeds; ICT-based agro-advisories; solar-dryer processing; agri-voltaic. Amber: reduced tillage; state-endorsed low-toxicity pesticides; nitrification-inhibitor urea; low-carbon substitution (electric tractor, robotics); soil-testing / controlled-release fertilizer; conservation tillage. Red: "Investments that lead to genetic erosion. Crops and vegetable varieties and seeds that replace traditional varieties"; over-exploitation; over-use of artificial fertilisers/pesticides; non-compliant EIA; "Rice and flour mills that produce GHG emissions".

#### 3.2.11 Fertiliser & Insecticides (§1.10, pp. 63–65)
Red list is prescriptive (verbatim): "Production of pesticides restricted by the Government of Nepal, for example, Aldrin, BHC, Chlordane, DDT. Refer to: List of Restricted Pesticides".

#### 3.2.12 Financial Intermediation (§11.1, pp. 128–130)
Red (verbatim): "Any Banking and Financial institutions (insurance of fossil fuel-based activities), capital markets (merchant banks), insurance companies and pension funds, trust funds or other funds activities that are carbon intensive, have environmentally poor standards, and cause significant damage to human health, climate, and ecosystems or categorised under excluded/ red are considered as financing in red activities."

## 5. Reporting requirements

### 5.1 Annex 4a — Regulator monitoring checklist (pp. 142–143)
Five assessment areas: Governance, Capacity, Adherence to Green Taxonomy, Financial Disclosure, Non-financial Disclosure. Each with 2–3 checklist items. Verbatim examples: "Have the BFIs, Insurance companies, merchant banks, and other relevant market actors set up the governance mechanism for the application of green finance taxonomy, e.g., sub-committee in the board, dedicated unit, and staff?" ; "Is the financial disclosure report made publicly available?"

### 5.2 Annex 4b — NRB SIS Green Finance Statement (p. 144)
Template: **"Green Finance Statement of Sector (Purpose) wise Loans and Advances (Annual) – Annual report template of NRB Supervisory Information System"**. Columns: Industry/Sector · Class A · Class B · Class C · Other Total · Grand Total (in NPR million). Rows = the 17 sectors verbatim above.

**This is the mandated aggregate submission for BFIs.** It is a *sector-level roll-up of loans and advances*, not a per-loan disclosure. NRB does **not** require per-loan or per-borrower taxonomy classification to be disclosed on the SIS return. Instead: "Nepal's green finance taxonomy is principally designed for financial institutions to classify the assets in their lending and investment portfolios and report to regulators for internal risk management and supervisory purposes." (s. 3.5.3, p. 32).

Table 4 (p. 32) — BFI reporting requirement (verbatim):
> "Integration in Supervisory Information System (SIS), which will separately label green finance for the lending areas in the system. annual financial reporting building on the existing reporting system. Refer to Annex 4b for more detail."

### 5.3 Annex 4c — SEBON Green Finance Statement (Capital Markets, p. 145)
Sixteen rows: Commercial Banks, Development Banks, Finance Company, Micro-Finance, Nepal Infrastructure Bank Limited, Life Insurance, Non-Life Insurance, Hydro power, Production and Processing, Business, Hotel and Tourism, Mutual Fund, Rights Share, Promoter Share, Loan Agreement, Others.

### 5.4 Annex 4d — NIA Green Finance Statement (Insurance, p. 146)
Rows: 1. Life Insurance (Parametric/non-parametric) — 1.1 Crop, 2.2 Livestock, 2.3 Human; 2. Non-Life Insurance — 2.1 Business, 2.2 Home/Residence/Real estates, 2.3 Motor vehicles, 2.4 Other Green Insurance.

### 5.5 Annex 4e — Red Book / Economic Survey (p. 146)
MoF should categorize green investments in the Aid Management Information System and update green finance narrative in the annual economic survey.

### 5.6 Annex 4f — Non-Financial Disclosure Report (p. 147)
Three-row template: Industry/Sector · Amount of Green Finance Investments · Impact Reporting. Fields: describe activities & sectors; category under taxonomy; verification method; qualitative/quantitative benefits; corrective measures; lessons learnt. Explicit note: "This is the minimum reporting format".

### 5.7 Annex 4g — Benchmark checklist for national alignment (pp. 148–150)
Groups indicators under the four Core Principles. Not activity-specific but portfolio-level benchmarks (e.g. hydropower "Development of hydropower plants (MgWatt)"; "Replacement of traditional brick kilns (FCBTK) with modern improved brick kilns (zigzag kilns, and electric tunnel kilns)"; "Installation and expansion of charging stations (number)"; "Reduce forest loss and stop land cover conversion (ha)").

### 5.8 Impact reporting cross-reference (ICMA Green Bond Principles, s. 3.5.3, pp. 33–34)
NRB requires alignment with ICMA GBP for green-labelled instruments:
- Reporting at least annually.
- Focus on use of proceeds and expected environmental impacts.
- Report at project or portfolio-consolidated basis.
- Convert reporting units on standard conversion factors.
- Report specific core indicators with methodology used.
- If partial alignment, disclose partial-eligibility accounting.
- Guidance available for Renewable Energy, Energy Efficiency, Sustainable Water and Wastewater management, etc.
