# NFRS S1 & S2 — Sustainability Disclosure Requirements

**Source documents (both exposure drafts, April 2026):**

- FINAL NFRS S1 Exposure Draft — *General Requirements for Disclosure of Sustainability-related Financial Information*, ASB Nepal, 44 pp (paragraphs 1–86 + Appendices A–E).
- FINAL NFRS S2 Exposure Draft — *Climate-related Disclosures*, ASB Nepal, 43 pp (paragraphs 1–37 + Appendices A–C).

Issued by the Accounting Standards Board, Nepal (ASB Nepal / ICAN). Both are explicitly aligned to IFRS S1 and IFRS S2 (Preface, S1 p. 2 / S2 p. 2). Public comment window closes **06 June 2026 (Jestha 23, 2083)**. Feedback goes to secretariat@asbnepal.gov.np.

---

## 1. Effective dates and applicability

### 1.1 Mandatory date — NOT YET SET

- NFRS S1 §E1 and NFRS S2 §C1 both read: *"An entity shall apply this Standard for annual reporting periods beginning on or after **XX XX 202X**. Earlier application is permitted."* (S1 p. 43; S2 p. 42)
- The mandatory application date is a literal placeholder in the exposure draft. **There is no legally binding effective date yet.** ASB Nepal will fix the date only when the final standards are issued after the June 2026 comment window.
- Early adoption is permitted, but §E1 / §C1 require the entity to apply S1 and S2 **together** if it adopts early.

### 1.2 Scope of application

Neither exposure draft carves out banks specifically or sets asset thresholds. NFRS S1 §5 states: *"An entity shall apply this Standard in preparing and reporting sustainability-related financial disclosures in accordance with Nepal Sustainability Disclosure Standards."* §8 makes S1 applicable regardless of whether the entity uses NFRS Accounting Standards or another GAAP. §9 explicitly extends applicability to profit-oriented entities (including public-sector business entities) and permits not-for-profits to apply with terminology adjustments.

That means, on the face of the standards, **every reporting entity in Nepal is in scope** once the effective date lands — the phased-adoption question (banks first, tiered by asset size, etc.) will come from the regulator (NRB, SEBON, Insurance Authority) as they operationalise. NRB has flagged intent to reference NFRS for bank sustainability disclosures but has not yet published a phased-adoption circular.

### 1.3 Transition reliefs (both S1 and S2)

- **No comparatives in Year 1** (S1 §E3, S2 §C3): comparative info not required in the first annual reporting period.
- **Climate-first relief** (S1 §E5–E6): in Year 1 an entity may disclose only climate-related information (i.e. apply S2 and apply S1 only insofar as it supports S2). If used, no S1 comparatives in Year 1 and no non-climate comparatives in Year 2.
- **Late-file relief** (S1 §E4): sustainability disclosures may be published after the financial statements — at the next half-year interim report or within 9 months of the year-end, whichever comes first.
- **GHG method relief** (S2 §C4(a)): an entity may keep using its pre-existing GHG measurement method (i.e. not Greenhouse Gas Protocol 2004) in Year 1.
- **Scope 3 relief** (S2 §C4(b)): **Scope 3 is not required in Year 1** — including "the additional information about its financed emissions" for banks/insurers/asset managers. §C5 lets an entity carry the relief forward for comparatives in later periods.

### 1.4 Exposure draft vs. final standard — implications for the demo

The current text is an *exposure draft* — subject to comment and revision. Word-for-word, ASB Nepal has copy-pasted IFRS S1 / S2 and re-labelled "IFRS" as "NFRS". Paragraph numbering matches the IFRS originals (S1 has 86 paragraphs + A–E; S2 has 37 paragraphs + A–C). Structural, substantive divergence from IFRS S1/S2 is essentially zero. **Demo claim check:** describing the platform as "NFRS S1 / S2-aligned" is factually accurate; describing it as helping banks "meet mandatory NFRS S2 disclosures" is currently overclaiming — mandatory date is still `XX XX 202X`. Preferred phrasing: *"aligned with NFRS S1 and S2 (Nepal's IFRS-baseline sustainability standards, ASB Nepal exposure draft April 2026)."*

## 2. NFRS S1 — general sustainability disclosures

### 2.1 Structure

S1 follows the ISSB/TCFD four-pillar architecture (§25):

- (a) **Governance** — §§26–27
- (b) **Strategy** — §§28–42
- (c) **Risk management** — §§43–44
- (d) **Metrics and targets** — §§45–53

Plus overarching conceptual foundations (§§10–24: fair presentation, materiality, reporting entity, connected information), general requirements (§§54–73: sources of guidance, location, timing, comparatives, statement of compliance), and judgements/uncertainties/errors (§§74–86).

### 2.2 Every disclosure required annually

**Governance (§27):**
- (a) The body(s) or individual(s) responsible for oversight of sustainability risks/opportunities, including:
  - (i) how responsibilities are reflected in terms of reference, mandates, role descriptions and policies;
  - (ii) how appropriate skills/competencies are determined and developed;
  - (iii) how and how often the body is informed;
  - (iv) how the body considers sustainability in strategy, major transactions, risk management (including trade-offs);
  - (v) how the body oversees target-setting and monitors progress (including whether metrics are in remuneration).
- (b) Management's role, including delegation to a specific management-level position/committee and controls/procedures used.

**Strategy (§§29–42):**
- §30 Sustainability risks and opportunities — description, time horizons (short/medium/long term), definitions of those horizons.
- §32 Business model and value chain — current/anticipated effects, geographic/facility/asset-type concentration.
- §33 Strategy and decision-making — how the entity has responded and plans to respond, progress on prior-year plans (quant + qual), trade-offs considered.
- §§34–40 Financial position, performance and cash flows — current and anticipated financial effects, investment/disposal plans, sources of funding, quantitative effects (single amount or range OK).
- §§41–42 Resilience — qualitative (and quantitative if applicable) resilience assessment, method, time horizon.

**Risk management (§44):** Processes to identify, assess, prioritise and monitor sustainability risks, including data sources, scenario-analysis use, materiality thresholds, prioritisation relative to other risks, monitoring, and year-over-year changes; same for opportunities; integration into overall enterprise risk management.

**Metrics and targets (§§45–53):**
- §46 Metrics required by any applicable NSDS + entity's own metrics tracking each risk/opportunity and its performance.
- §48 Industry-common metrics.
- §§49–50 If a metric is taken from a non-NSDS source, disclose the source; if internally developed, disclose definition, whether absolute/relative/RAG, third-party validation, calculation method and limitations.
- §51 For each target: metric used, target value, period, base period, milestones/interim targets, performance vs. target, revisions and rationale.

### 2.3 Materiality (§§17–19, B13–B37)

Information is material if omitting/misstating/obscuring it could reasonably be expected to influence primary-user decisions (§18). Materiality is entity-specific (§14). Judgement required (§75(c)). Primary users are existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors (Appendix A).

### 2.4 Comparability (§§70–71)

Prior-period comparative information required for all amounts (§70). Narrative comparatives if useful. Prior-period errors must be corrected by restatement unless impracticable (§§83–86, B55–B59).

### 2.5 Assurance / audit

**No standalone assurance requirement in the exposure draft.** S1 does not impose an audit or independent assurance mandate. Assurance regime will come from a separate ICAN pronouncement or NRB/SEBON regulation. §50(c) does require an entity to disclose whether an entity-developed metric is validated by a third party and, if so, which party (this is disclosure about assurance, not a mandate to seek it).

### 2.6 Statement of compliance (§72)

An entity may only claim NFRS-compliant disclosures if it complies with **all** requirements of NSDS. §73 preserves the claim of compliance where a law prohibits disclosure or where opportunity information is commercially sensitive (§§B33–B37 detail the commercially-sensitive exemption).

## 3. NFRS S2 — climate-related disclosures

### 3.1 Structure

Same four-pillar TCFD architecture (§§5–37):

- **Governance** — §§5–7 (near-verbatim to S1 §§26–27, climate-scoped).
- **Strategy** — §§8–23 (climate risks/opportunities, transition plan, financial effects, climate resilience, scenario analysis).
- **Risk management** — §§24–26.
- **Metrics and targets** — §§27–37.

### 3.2 Scope (§3)

Applies to (a) climate physical risks (acute + chronic), (b) climate transition risks, and (c) climate opportunities.

### 3.3 Scope 1, 2, 3 emissions — verbatim (§29(a))

An entity shall:

- **(i)** disclose absolute gross Scope 1, Scope 2 and Scope 3 GHG emissions in tCO₂e (§29(a)(i), pp. 14–15);
- **(ii)** measure emissions per the **Greenhouse Gas Protocol Corporate Standard (2004)**, unless a jurisdictional authority or exchange requires a different method (§29(a)(ii); §§B23–B25);
- **(iii)** disclose the measurement approach, inputs and assumptions, changes made during the period, and rationale (§29(a)(iii); §§B26–B29);
- **(iv)** disaggregate Scope 1 and Scope 2 between (1) the consolidated group and (2) other investees (associates, JVs, unconsolidated subs) (§29(a)(iv));
- **(v)** disclose **location-based Scope 2** and information about contractual instruments if any (§29(a)(v); §§B30–B31) — market-based Scope 2 is permitted supplementary, not mandatory;
- **(vi)** for Scope 3: disclose the categories included (all 15 GHG Protocol Scope 3 categories considered), plus additional Category 15 / financed-emissions info if the entity is in asset management, commercial banking or insurance (§29(a)(vi); §§B32–B57 for measurement framework; §§B58–B63 for financed emissions).

### 3.4 Scope 3 Category 15 (financed emissions) — specific requirements

**§29(a)(vi)(2)** requires additional financed-emissions disclosure when the entity is in asset management, commercial banking or insurance. Detailed rules in §§B58–B63:

- **§B58** frames financed emissions as an exposure indicator to climate transition/physical risk feeding credit, market, reputational and operational risk.
- **§B59** carves out three sub-industries: asset management (B61), commercial banking (B62), insurance (B63).
- **§B60** all Scope-3 GHG requirements in §29(a) apply to financed emissions.

**§B62 Commercial banking** (this is the one that matters for the Jana platform):

- **(a)** Absolute gross financed emissions, disaggregated by Scope 1 / Scope 2 / Scope 3 **for each industry by asset class**, where:
  - **(i) Industry** — the entity shall use the **Global Industry Classification Standard (GICS) 6-digit industry-level code** for classifying counterparties (latest version at reporting date).
  - **(ii) Asset class** — must include **loans, project finance, bonds, equity investments, and undrawn loan commitments**. Additional asset classes may be added with rationale.
- **(b)** Gross exposure to each industry × asset class, in the entity's presentation currency; funded amounts = carrying amount before loss allowance; undrawn commitments disclosed separately from drawn.
- **(c)** Percentage of gross exposure included in the calculation; if <100 %, disclose exclusions by asset type; exclude risk-mitigant impacts from funded amounts; disclose separately the % of undrawn commitments included.
- **(d)** **Methodology** — the method of allocation used to attribute the entity's share of emissions in relation to the size of gross exposure.

### 3.5 PCAF methodology references

**PCAF is not named anywhere in NFRS S2.** The word "PCAF" does not appear in the exposure draft. This is consistent with IFRS S2 (which also stops short of prescribing PCAF by name).

However, the Scope 3 measurement framework in §§B38–B57 is functionally equivalent to PCAF's data-quality hierarchy — it just doesn't cite it. §B40 mandates prioritisation of inputs in this order:

1. **Direct measurement** (§§B43–B45) — direct monitoring of GHG emissions.
2. **Data from specific activities in the entity's value chain (primary data)** (§§B46–B49) — supplier meter readings, utility bills, supplier-specific emission factors.
3. **Timely data that faithfully represents the jurisdiction and technology** of the activity (§§B50–B52).
4. **Verified data** (§§B53–B54).

Secondary data (industry-average, proxy data) is permitted but the entity must consider how faithfully it represents its activity (§B49). This is the PCAF Score 1 (verified emissions) → Score 5 (economic-activity-based proxy) ladder in different language.

Banks operationally can (and should) use PCAF to comply — NFRS S2 leaves the specific method open (§B62(d) says only "the methodology the entity used to calculate its financed emissions, including the method of allocation the entity used to attribute its share of emissions in relation to the size of its gross exposure"). PCAF is the obvious choice because it satisfies §29(a)(iii) (disclose approach/inputs/assumptions) and the §§B40 hierarchy in one framework.

### 3.6 Data-quality disclosure — PCAF score explicitly required?

**No — NFRS S2 does not require disclosure of a PCAF data-quality score.** What it does require:

- §29(a)(iii): disclose measurement approach, inputs and assumptions.
- §§B55–B56: disclose the extent to which Scope 3 emissions are measured using (a) inputs from specific value-chain activities, and (b) verified inputs.
- §B57: if the entity determines it is impracticable to estimate Scope 3, it must disclose how it is managing Scope 3 emissions instead.

A PCAF score (1–5) is one clean way to satisfy §§B55–B56. Not mandatory. **For the demo, saying "NFRS S2 requires a PCAF score" is overclaiming;** saying "NFRS S2 requires banks to disclose the quality/verification level of their financed-emissions inputs; PCAF is the standard convention for that disclosure" is accurate.

### 3.7 Scenario analysis (§22, §§B1–B18)

- Required **annually** as a resilience assessment (§22).
- Scenario analysis itself can be done less frequently, aligned with the strategic-planning cycle (e.g. every 3–5 years) (§B18).
- Must consider at least one climate-related scenario **aligned with the latest international agreement on climate change** (Paris) (§22(b)(i)(4)).
- Approach must be **commensurate with the entity's circumstances** — a bank with low climate exposure and limited resources may use a qualitative narrative approach; a bank with high exposure and access to resources is expected to apply a more quantitative approach (§§B4–B7, B17).
- Required disclosures (§22(a)–(b)): assessment as at reporting date, uncertainties, capacity to adjust, scenarios used and sources, whether a diverse range was used, whether scenarios covered transition vs. physical, time horizons, scope of operations, key assumptions (climate policy, macro trends, national/regional variables, energy mix, technology), reporting period in which scenario analysis was carried out.

### 3.8 Transition plan (§14(a)(iv))

If the entity has a climate transition plan, it must disclose:

- Key assumptions and dependencies underpinning the plan.
- How the entity plans to achieve any GHG targets (§14(a)(v), §§33–36).
- Current and anticipated changes to business model, resource allocation, mitigation/adaptation efforts (§14(a)(i)–(iii)).
- How the entity is resourcing, and plans to resource, these activities (§14(b)).
- Progress against previously-disclosed plans (§14(c)).

There is **no requirement to have a transition plan** — but if the entity has one, disclosure is mandatory.

### 3.9 Metrics and targets (§§27–37) — cross-industry metric categories (§29)

Beyond the GHG disclosures already covered, §29(b)–(g) require:

- **(b) Transition risk** — amount and % of assets or business activities vulnerable to climate transition risks.
- **(c) Physical risk** — amount and % of assets/activities vulnerable to physical risks.
- **(d) Climate opportunities** — amount and % of assets/activities aligned with climate opportunities.
- **(e) Capital deployment** — capex, financing or investment deployed toward climate-related risks and opportunities.
- **(f) Internal carbon prices** — whether and how the entity applies a carbon price in decision-making; the price per tCO₂e.
- **(g) Remuneration** — whether and how climate is factored into executive remuneration; % of executive remuneration linked to climate.

**Targets (§§33–36):** For each climate-related target — metric used, objective (mitigation/adaptation/science-based), part of entity to which it applies, period, base period, milestones/interim targets, absolute vs. intensity, alignment with the Paris Agreement. Approach to setting/reviewing targets, third-party validation, monitoring, revisions. Performance vs. target with trend analysis.

**For GHG-emissions targets specifically (§36):** which gases, which scopes, gross vs. net (net target requires the associated gross target too), whether sectoral decarbonisation approach was used, planned use of carbon credits (extent, third-party verifier, type — nature-based vs. technological, reduction vs. removal, permanence assumptions).

## 4. Financed emissions specifics — the platform-critical section

Consolidating §§B58–B63 plus §§29(a) and §§B38–B57 as they apply to a commercial bank:

### 4.1 What must be measured

Absolute gross financed emissions in tCO₂e, disaggregated by:
- Scope 1 / Scope 2 / Scope 3 of the counterparty;
- Industry (GICS 6-digit) of the counterparty;
- Asset class of the exposure (loans, project finance, bonds, equity, undrawn commitments — at minimum).

### 4.2 Attribution methodology

- The bank must disclose **the method of allocation used to attribute its share of the counterparty's emissions in relation to the size of its gross exposure** (§B62(d)).
- No specific attribution formula prescribed. PCAF's outstanding-amount-over-EVIC (listed equity/bonds) and outstanding-loan-over-total-equity-and-debt (business loans) formulas are the industry norm and satisfy §B62(d).
- The Scope 3 measurement framework §§B40–B54 governs input prioritisation — direct measurement > primary activity data > jurisdiction/tech-appropriate > verified.

### 4.3 Asset classes covered

Mandatory minimum (§B62(a)(ii)): **loans, project finance, bonds, equity investments, undrawn loan commitments.** Additional classes optional but must be justified.

### 4.4 Data quality reporting

- §29(a)(iii): disclose approach, inputs, assumptions.
- §§B55–B56: disclose the extent to which the calculation uses (a) inputs from specific value-chain activities, and (b) verified inputs.
- §B62(c): disclose the % of gross exposure included; if <100 %, explain exclusions; separately disclose the % of undrawn commitments included.
- No mandatory PCAF-score-band disclosure. PCAF scoring is the practical way to satisfy §§B55–B56.

### 4.5 Historical baselines

- Comparatives not required in Year 1 (§C3).
- After Year 1, comparatives required per §70 of S1.
- Base period for each target must be disclosed (§33(e)).
- Consistency of metric definitions over time is required (§52 of S1) — redefinitions trigger §B52 of S1.

### 4.6 Target setting

§§33–36 apply to financed-emissions targets exactly as to any climate target. Key items: scope coverage, absolute vs. intensity, gross vs. net, sectoral decarbonisation approach, carbon credit reliance, and Paris-alignment. Third-party validation of the target methodology must be disclosed if applicable (§34(a)).

## 5. Data fields required for compliance

Grouped by disclosure topic. This is the data-model requirement for the Jana platform.

### 5.1 Entity-level (governance / strategy)

- Governance body name(s), individuals, terms of reference, mandate, meeting cadence.
- Skills/competency register for board sustainability oversight.
- Management-level position/committee with delegated sustainability oversight.
- Remuneration policy fields: % of exec pay linked to sustainability / climate metrics.
- Definitions of short-, medium- and long-term horizons (in years) and link to planning cycle.
- Business model description; value chain map with concentration points (geo, facility, asset).
- Transition plan document; key assumptions and dependencies.
- Financial planning integration — capex, opex, funding sources linked to sustainability actions.
- Scenario analysis: scenarios used, sources, whether transition/physical/diverse, whether Paris-aligned, time horizons, scope, assumptions (policy, macro, national variables, energy mix, tech), reporting period of analysis.
- Resilience assessment narrative + optional quantitative range.
- Statement of compliance (yes/no).
- Judgements register (material judgements affecting disclosures).
- Measurement uncertainty register (amounts subject to high uncertainty + sources + assumptions).
- Prior-period error log (with restatement disclosures).

### 5.2 Portfolio-level (financed emissions and exposure metrics)

For each counterparty × asset class combination:
- Counterparty identifier + GICS 6-digit industry code.
- Asset class (loans / project finance / bonds / equity / undrawn commitments / other with rationale).
- Gross exposure in NPR (funded carrying amount before loss allowance; undrawn commitments separate).
- Counterparty Scope 1 emissions (tCO₂e).
- Counterparty Scope 2 emissions (tCO₂e), location-based (market-based optional).
- Counterparty Scope 3 emissions (tCO₂e), with categories disclosed.
- Attribution factor (share of counterparty attributed to the bank).
- Attributed emissions per scope.
- Data source per emission input (direct / primary / secondary / proxy).
- Data quality band per input (Score 1–5 PCAF convention — implicit).
- Verification status per input (verified / unverified).
- Reporting period / vintage of the input.
- Emission factor used (source + jurisdiction).
- Impaired vs. performing status (does not change gross exposure but is a disclosure input).

### 5.3 Portfolio-level (climate risk exposure metrics)

- % of assets vulnerable to transition risk (with risk tag).
- % of assets vulnerable to physical risk (with hazard tag: flood / drought / heat / storm / sea-level).
- % of assets aligned with climate opportunities (with alignment basis — e.g. NRB Green Finance Taxonomy).
- Capex/financing deployed to climate-related risks and opportunities (NPR, with tag).
- Internal carbon price (NPR/tCO₂e) and business applications where used.

### 5.4 Target-tracking

For each target:
- Metric definition; objective (mitigation / adaptation / SBTi-conformance); scope covered; entity/business unit covered; period; base period; milestones/interim targets; absolute vs. intensity; gross vs. net; sectoral decarbonisation flag; carbon-credit reliance % + verifier + credit type; Paris alignment description; third-party validation; performance history; revisions log.

### 5.5 Metric metadata

For each metric:
- Source (NSDS / SASB / GRI / ESRS / internal); definition; absolute / relative / RAG; third-party validation status; calculation method; inputs; limitations; significant assumptions.

## 6. Cross-reference to Nepal's other frameworks

- **NRB ESRM Guideline (2022)** — **not cited** in either NFRS S1 or S2. The only Nepali institutions named are ASB Nepal, ICAN, the Ministry of Finance, Ministry of Forests and Environment, NRB, Nepal Insurance Authority, Electricity Regulatory Commission, SEBON, and the Office of the Company Registrar — and those appear only in the Preface as members of the NSRS Development Committee (S1 p. 5 / S2 p. 5). ESRM operates as a separate NRB circular; NFRS does not defer to or require ESRM inputs. Practically, the bank's ESRM output (E&S risk categorisation, ESDD findings) is a natural input to §22 (resilience) and §29(b)–(c) (transition and physical risk vulnerability metrics), but the linkage is at the bank's discretion, not standard-mandated.
- **NRB Nepal Green Finance Taxonomy (2024)** — **not cited.** Taxonomy alignment is left to bank judgement. §29(d) requires disclosure of the % of assets aligned with climate opportunities but does not prescribe a taxonomy; the Nepal Green Finance Taxonomy is the obvious anchor for a Nepali bank.
- **PCAF** — **not cited.** See §3.5.
- **IFRS S1 / S2, TCFD, SASB, GHG Protocol, ISSB, IPCC** — all cited or referenced. NFRS S2 explicitly requires GHG Protocol Corporate Standard 2004 (§29(a)(ii)); Scope 3 measurement per GHG Protocol Corporate Value Chain 2011 (§29(a)(vi)(1), Appendix A); IPCC latest assessment for global-warming-potential values (§§B21–B22); "Industry-based Guidance on Implementing NFRS S2" (i.e. SASB-derived) for industry-based metrics (§§12, 23, 32).

**Implication for cross-framework mapping in the demo:** NFRS S2 is deliberately silent on the Nepal-specific stack. The linkage from ESRM → Taxonomy → NFRS S2 → PCAF is a Jana editorial choice, not a regulator-mandated crosswalk. Presenting it in the demo is fine and useful — just don't state that NFRS S2 requires it.

## 7. Reporting format

- **Location (§§60–63):** Disclosures form part of the entity's **general purpose financial reports** — most commonly the management commentary, management report, MD&A, integrated report or strategic report accompanying the annual financial statements. Not a standalone document by requirement. Cross-references to other published reports are permitted subject to §§B45–B47.
- **Timing (§§64–66):** Same reporting period as the financial statements; issued at the same time (with Year 1 late-file relief per §E4).
- **Content mix:** Narrative + tables + quantitative amounts. §36 and §51 imply target tables with a defined column set; §29 and §B62 imply a financed-emissions matrix (industry × asset class × scope). Quantitative disclosures may be single amounts or ranges (§36, §17, §41 of S1).
- **Currency:** Same presentation currency as the related financial statements (§24 of S1).
- **Templates:** **No ICAN-issued template is bundled with the exposure drafts.** ASB Nepal has committed to "Industry-based Guidance on Implementing NFRS S2" (§§12, 23, 32) — the SASB-derived industry supplement — but that is guidance, not a form. Preparers therefore build their own tables to satisfy §B62 (financed emissions), §51 of S1 (targets) and §29(b)–(g) (cross-industry metrics).
- **Statement of compliance (§72 of S1):** required, explicit and unreserved. An entity can only make the statement if every applicable NSDS requirement is met.
- **Comparatives:** required from Year 2 onward (§70 of S1); errors restated (§§83–86 of S1).

## Bottom line for the Jana platform

- The demo's NFRS disclosure roll-up should be labelled as an **exposure-draft-aligned** capability — the mandatory date is still `XX XX 202X`.
- For a Nepali commercial bank, the material NFRS S2 obligations that touch the Jana data model are the **financed-emissions matrix (industry GICS × asset class × Scope 1/2/3)**, **gross exposure per cell**, **coverage percentage**, and **methodology / attribution factor / data-quality disclosure**. Everything else (governance, scenario, targets, transition plan) is prose plus a small structured layer.
- PCAF is not mandated but is the industry-standard way to satisfy §B62(d) attribution and §§B55–B56 data-quality disclosure.
- The demo's cross-framework story (ESRM → Taxonomy → NFRS → PCAF) is legitimate as a Jana editorial framing but should never be presented as a NFRS-mandated chain.
